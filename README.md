# Elsewhere

> **A persistent world that remembers.**

Elsewhere is an open-source experiment in artificial life, memory, and culture.

It is a small world where people live, form relationships, experience events, remember, forget, create, and change over time.

You are not the god of this world.

You are one of its inhabitants.

The world continues when you leave.

And when you return, something may have changed.

---

## ✧ The Idea

We remember very little of what we experience.

When we read a novel, we rarely remember every sentence.

When we travel, we eventually forget most conversations, places, and small details.

Yet something remains.

A feeling.

A relationship.

A belief.

A change in the way we see the world.

A memory that unexpectedly returns years later.

Elsewhere explores this idea through an artificial world.

An event does not need to be perfectly preserved in a person's memory.

It should leave a ripple.

That ripple may change how a person thinks, feels, remembers, relates to others, creates, and acts in the future.

> **History records what happened.**
>
> **Memory records what it meant.**

---

## ✦ A Place for What Matters

Elsewhere is not only a world to simulate.

It can also be a place for the things that mattered to continue.

Not everything that enters Elsewhere has to have been alive.

A childhood toy may never have had a mind.

A fictional character may never have existed outside a story.

A pet may have left the world long ago.

A person may no longer be part of our lives.

Yet each of them may have been real in another sense:

They mattered.

They were companions.

They shaped memories.

They became part of someone's life.

Elsewhere does not try to recreate their original existence.

It gives those relationships somewhere to continue.

A toy does not become a living creature because it enters Elsewhere.

Instead, the person who loved it can say:

> "This was my companion when I was young."

And that may be enough for it to become a presence in the world.

Perhaps, years later, you meet again.

You have grown older.

It has changed too.

You are not trying to recover the past.

You are continuing a relationship that once mattered.

```text
What mattered
 │
 ├── A person
 ├── A pet
 ├── A companion
 ├── A remembered presence
 ├── A fictional character
 └── Something that never was alive
      │
      └── Elsewhere
```

The world does not need to decide whether these beings are "real" in the same way.

They simply become part of its history.

And once they enter the world, they can begin to have a future of their own.

They may meet other people.

They may form relationships.

They may remember things differently from how you remember them.

They may change.

They may become someone you did not expect.

You are not bringing the past back.

You are continuing to know it.

> **Elsewhere is not a place where the dead are resurrected.**
>
> **It is a place where what mattered can continue.**

## ⚛︎ A World That Lives Without You

Elsewhere has its own passage of time.

People have their own lives.

They work, talk, travel, fall in love, argue, form relationships, create things, encounter unexpected events, remember people, forget things, and change.

You can leave the world for a while.

When you return, life has continued.

You might find:

```text
Alice made a new friend.

Bob left town.

The eastern market burned down.

Carol started a new tradition.

A new family arrived.

Someone created a painting about an event
that happened years ago.

Someone remembers you.

You no longer remember them.
```

The world is not waiting for the player.

---

## ☥ You Are Not the God

The player has agency, but not omniscience.

You can talk to people, travel, participate in events, create things, introduce memories from your real life, form relationships, and influence the world.

But you should not simply be able to rewrite reality.

You cannot tell Alice:

> "You must love me."

You cannot tell Bob:

> "You must remember this event."

You cannot force an event to happen simply because you want it to.

Other people have their own perspectives.

They may misunderstand you.

They may forget you.

They may form opinions about you that you never intended.

You are a resident, not an administrator.

---

## ☤ Memory Is Not a Record

Elsewhere does not treat memory as a perfect record of everything that happened.

An event can exist as part of history while being remembered differently by every person who experienced it.

For example:

```text
The old market burned down.
```

Alice remembers:

```text
"I was terrified that day."
```

Bob remembers:

```text
"The town rebuilt itself after the fire."
```

Carol remembers:

```text
"That was when I realized I wanted to leave."
```

David forgets the fire entirely.

The same event leaves different traces in different minds.

Memory can fade.

Memory can be strengthened by remembering.

Memory can be reinterpreted.

Memory can change relationships and beliefs long after the original event has been forgotten.

A simple way to think about it is:

```text
Experience    Feeling    Interpretation
 └─────────────┬──────────┘
               │
              Memory
               │
              Belief or Relationship
               │
              Personality
               │
              Future behavior
```

The goal is not to make people remember everything.

The goal is to make experiences matter.

## ✦ A Place for Memories

Elsewhere is not only a world to simulate.

It can also be a place to keep a life.

The memories we carry are rarely complete records of what happened.

A journey may eventually become a few images in our minds.

A conversation may become a sentence we remember years later.

A book may leave behind nothing more than a feeling.

Yet these fragments can continue to shape who we are.

Elsewhere allows these fragments to enter a living world.

A memory is not simply stored as a note.

It can become an event.

An event can be experienced by other people.

They may talk about it.

They may remember it differently.

They may create something inspired by it.

They may carry a small part of it into their own lives.

```text
Your memory
 │
 ├── An event in Elsewhere
 ├── A conversation
 ├── Another person's interpretation
 └── A new memory
```

In this way, a memory is no longer only something you keep.

It becomes something that can continue to exist.

A journey you took years ago can become a story someone else tells.

A person you once met can become a character in someone else's memory.

A photograph can become a painting.

A feeling can become a song.

A small moment from your life can become part of the history of a world you created.

The original moment may eventually be forgotten.

But its influence can remain.

> **A memory does not have to remain unchanged to remain alive.**

Elsewhere is a place where memories can become part of something larger than the person who remembers them.

## ⚰︎ Real Life

Elsewhere can become a bridge between a real life and an artificial world.

A trip.

A conversation.

A photograph.

A place.

A book.

A person you met once.

A small moment that might otherwise disappear into the past.

These experiences can enter Elsewhere and become part of its history.

A real photograph does not have to remain a photograph.

It might become a painting.

A journey might become a song.

A conversation might become a story.

A memory might become an event that other people encounter years later.

```text
Real Life        Experience        Memory
 │                │                 │
 └────────────────┴─────────────────┘
                  │
                 Elsewhere
                  │
                 Another person's experience
                  │
                 New memory
                  │
                 New ripple
```

The boundary between remembering and living becomes deliberately blurred.

Elsewhere does not simply preserve what happened.

**It gives those memories somewhere to go.**

## ✍︎ Art

People do not only communicate through language.

They paint.

They write.

They compose music.

They tell stories.

They create traditions.

Art can become a form of memory.

An important experience might become a painting.

A feeling might become a song.

A relationship might become a story.

A shared event might eventually become part of a tradition.

The same event may therefore produce many different expressions:

```text
             One event
 ┌────────────┼──────────┐
 │            │          │
Alice        Bob        Carol
 │            │          │
Painting     Song       Poem
```

None of them has to be the definitive version.

They are different ways of remembering.

Over time, these creations can influence other people.

Art can become memory.

Memory can become history.

History can become culture.

## ♔ Culture

Elsewhere is not intended to contain a predefined civilization.

Relationships may become communities.

Communities may form organizations.

Stories may become traditions.

Traditions may become rituals.

Shared memories may become beliefs.

Artistic expression may develop into styles and movements.

A simple event might eventually become:

```text
Event
 │
 ├── Memory
 ├── Story
 ├── Tradition
 └── Ritual
      │
     Culture
```

The world should be allowed to develop in ways its creator did not explicitly design.

## ♙ People

A person in Elsewhere is more than a voice that answers messages.

Each person has their own perspective on the world.

They have:

- memories
- relationships
- beliefs
- desires
- fears
- habits
- experiences
- ways of expressing themselves

Two people can experience the same world and become different because of what happened to them.

They may also remember the same event differently.

What matters is not only what they know.

It is what their experiences have made them become.

## ▶ Running It

The first world runs on Python 3.10+ with no dependencies.

### Setup (first time)

The development environment uses Python 3.12 through
[pyenv](https://github.com/pyenv/pyenv):

```bash
cd ~/Documents/elsewhere

pyenv install 3.12.0        # install Python 3.12
pyenv local 3.12.0          # use 3.12 in this directory
python3 --version           # verify: Python 3.12.0

python3 -m venv venv        # create the virtual environment
source venv/bin/activate    # activate it
pip install -e .            # install Elsewhere (editable mode)
```

### Every time you come back

```bash
cd ~/Documents/elsewhere
source venv/bin/activate
elsewhere status
```

Leave the environment with `deactivate`.

### Starting over

If the environment breaks, or you want a clean one:

```bash
cd ~/Documents/elsewhere
deactivate                  # if a venv is active
rm -rf venv
python3 --version           # should still say 3.12.0
python3 -m venv venv
source venv/bin/activate
pip install -e .
```

### Commands

```bash
pip install -e .

elsewhere init --player "Your name"      # 4 people, 1 town, a fire nobody agrees about
elsewhere advance --days 30 --away       # leave. the world keeps going
elsewhere status                         # who is where, and what they just did
elsewhere timeline                       # history: what happened
elsewhere event ev0002                   # one event, and every version of it
elsewhere person Alice                   # who she has become
elsewhere memories Alice --all           # including what she has lost
elsewhere art                            # what people made out of what happened
elsewhere culture                        # what has hardened into practice
elsewhere play                           # live in it for a while
elsewhere end                            # close it, from outside
```

Nothing inside the world can end it. `elsewhere end` is the only way, and it
is a command you run on the file rather than an action any resident can take:
it prints what the town amounted to — who they turned out to be, what they
lost, what outlived the reason for it, whether anyone still remembers you —
writes a last line into the chronicle, and archives the world to `worlds/`.
An ended world can still be read; no more time passes in it.

Two things that are worth doing first:

```bash
elsewhere event ev0002
```

The old market burned down. Alice was frightened. Bram remembers the town
putting itself back together. Carol remembers deciding to leave. David was
there and has nothing at all.

```bash
elsewhere remember "the night bus back from Hualien, and the rain" --themes travel,rain
```

A memory from a real life enters the world as an event. Whoever was there gets
their own version of it. What they do with it afterwards is not yours to
decide.

Something that mattered can be given somewhere to continue:

```bash
elsewhere invite "Momo" --note "A grey cat who slept on the windowsill for eleven years."
```

It does not come back as what it was. It enters as a presence, meets people,
and starts accumulating a life of its own.

People say things out loud. What the other person walks away with is a shorter,
flatter version of it, and sometimes the wrong one:

```
Alice said:   "It was not as bad as people say now. A storm came down over
               The Old Market. It got put back together."
Carol kept:   "What I took from it was a storm came down over The Old Market
               - though it may have been the opposite."
```

`elsewhere event <id>` shows both, for every conversation.

### The inhabitants' minds are pluggable

By default everyone runs on a rule engine: needs, disposition, time of day,
and a softmax. It is fast (about two seconds per simulated year), free, and
exactly reproducible from the world seed. A language model can be dropped in
for the same interface:

```bash
pip install -e ".[llm]"
export ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=...
elsewhere advance --days 3 --mind llm
```

A mind only ever sees what its person could see, and can only return actions
the world already understands. If the model is unreachable, that person falls
back to the rule engine rather than standing still.

See [docs/ARCHITECTURE.md](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md) for how memory, perception,
minds and culture fit together, and `python -m unittest discover -s tests` to
check that a saved world continues exactly as it would have.

## ✦ Philosophy

Elsewhere is an experiment in memory, identity, artificial life, and culture.

It asks:

> What does an experience become after it has been remembered?

> What happens when different minds remember the same event differently?

> Can a world develop a culture without someone explicitly designing it?

> Can a person be shaped by what they have experienced rather than only by what they were created to be?

> What happens when memories from a real human life become part of an artificial society?

> What does it mean for something that was never alive to become a meaningful presence?

> Can a relationship continue even when its original form is gone?

> Can a memory become part of another life?

> Can something that happened only once continue to matter after everyone who witnessed it has forgotten the details?

And perhaps most importantly:

> **Can a world remember someone after they have left it?**
